# You Are Not Your Anxious Thoughts
## The Witness Self • Sakshi

---

## The Storm Inside

It's 2 AM. Your chest is tight. Your mind is looping: *"I'm failing. I'm broken. Everyone knows I'm faking it."*

And the worst part? You believe it. Completely. Because in that moment, you don't *have* anxious thoughts—you *are* the anxiety. There's no separation. No space. Just you, drowning in your own mind.

But what if the drowning person and the ocean aren't the same thing?

---

## The Witness You've Never Met

Three thousand years ago, the Katha Upanishad revealed something radical. In the same teaching where Death taught Nachiketa about shreya and preya (Insight #1), he went deeper:

> *"There are two selves, the separate ego and the indivisible Atman. When one rises above I and me and mine, the Atman is revealed as one's real Self."*  
> — Katha Upanishad II.3.13 (Easwaran)

The Upanishads call this deeper self *Sakshi* (साक्षी)—the Witness.

Not a mystical concept. A practical reality you can test right now.

---

## The Teaching

Here's the proof: You're anxious. Thoughts are racing. And yet—*something in you is aware that thoughts are racing.*

That awareness isn't anxious. It's just… watching.

Think of it like a movie screen. The screen shows horror films, comedies, tragedies. But the screen itself? Untouched. Unchanged. The screen doesn't flinch during the scary parts.

Your thoughts are the movie. You are the screen.

In Insight #1, you learned that you can *choose* between shreya and preya. But who is the one choosing? Not the anxious thoughts—they just react. The chooser is the witness. The awareness behind the noise.

That's you. The real you. And it has never panicked once.

## The Practice: Thought Labeling

Next time anxiety hits, try this:

1. **Notice** the thought: *"I'm not good enough."*
2. **Label it silently:** *"Thinking, thinking, thinking."*
3. **Reframe:** Instead of "I am anxious," say: *"There is a thought about anxiety."*
4. **Notice the gap.** You just observed the thought. That means you're not the thought.

Don't fight the anxiety. Don't believe it. Just *watch* it—the way you'd watch clouds crossing the sky.

The clouds don't damage the sky. Your thoughts don't damage you.

**Try it right now.** Close your eyes for ten seconds. Notice whatever thought arises. Label it: *"Thinking."* Then open your eyes.

You just met the witness.
